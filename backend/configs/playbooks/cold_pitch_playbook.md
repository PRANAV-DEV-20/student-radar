# High-Signal Cold Outreach & Sponsor Pitch Playbook

## Strategic Objective
Craft a hyper-personalized, zero-fluff cold pitch to send directly to Angel Investors, Engineering Directors, Hackathon Judges, or Academic Lab PIs. Designed for sub-30-second mobile reading with an unmistakable proof-of-work anchor.

## 4-Paragraph Rule System

### Subject Line Formula
- Must be lowercase, specific, and metric-anchored.
- Examples:
  - `re: 12ms decentralized mesh coordination (proof-of-work dossier)`
  - `subterranean lora consensus: working prototype + benchmarks`
  - `question on darpa subterranean comms & px4 autopilot`

### Paragraph 1: Direct Relevance & Proof-of-Work Hook
- No pleasantries ("Hope this finds you well"). Start immediately with why you are reaching out to *them specifically*.
- Reference a paper, tweet, or investment they made: "Saw your recent post on distributed mesh resilience in GPS-denied environments."

### Paragraph 2: The Breakthrough & Concrete Metrics
- State what you built in exactly two sentences:
  - "My team developed [Project Title], an open-source [Category] that achieves [Core Breakthrough]."
  - State 2 verified benchmarks: "In our hardware field benchmarks, we recorded [Metric 1] and [Metric 2] across [Scale/Condition]."

### Paragraph 3: The Artifact Link
- Anchor your credibility with the public dossier:
  - "I documented the complete system architecture, failover diagrams, and reproducible benchmarks in our Proof-of-Work Dossier here: [Dossier URL]"
  - Include GitHub repository link and verification status.

### Paragraph 4: The Low-Friction Asymmetric Ask
- Never ask for "a 30-minute coffee chat". Ask for a binary technical critique or a specific connection:
  - "Would you be open to giving a 2-sentence critique on our consensus failover state machine?"
  - Or: "Are you attending [Event Name] next week? We would love to show you the physical hardware rig for 3 minutes."

### Sign-off
- Clean signature: Name, University/Affiliation, Verified Dossier Link, GitHub/Twitter handle.
